# AR Divergent Reasoning

1. Restate the decision or claim neutrally without adopting its conclusion.
2. Separate source-verified facts, inferences, assumptions, and unknowns. Keep
   missing or conflicting evidence visible.
3. Form a base case, then generate two to four alternatives with genuinely
   different causal mechanisms. Do not create cosmetic wording variants.
4. Give each case its strongest supporting evidence and the observation that
   would most clearly weaken or falsify it.
5. Check for prompt anchoring, consensus echo, selection bias, stale evidence,
   and a missing cautious or constructive direction.
6. Propose the smallest next tests that discriminate between cases. Prefer
   tests that can turn a favored explanation red.
7. Conclude with the current best-supported case, confidence limits, unresolved
   disagreements, and what new evidence could change the conclusion.

Do not oppose the user by default. Divergence must improve coverage or
falsifiability. Never turn this workflow into trading instructions, and never
make win-rate, alpha, or expectation claims below the repository's independent
causal-cluster threshold.
